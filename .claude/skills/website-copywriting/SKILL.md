# Website copywriting

Goal: copy that gets read, believed, and acted on — not generic filler
("we provide innovative solutions for all your needs"). Pairs with
`pro-website-builder` (visual tone) and `lead-magnet-builder` (opt-in copy).

## Process

1. **Get the one-sentence brief before writing anything.** Who is this for,
   what's the single core promise, what action should they take next? If the
   user hasn't said, ask or infer from context — don't write generic copy to
   fill space.
2. **Hero formula first.** Every page needs: headline (the outcome/promise,
   not the product category), subheadline (who it's for / how it works, one
   sentence), CTA (specific action, not "Submit"). Get this right before
   writing body copy — it's what 80% of visitors actually read.
3. **Pick a structure for the body**, don't just write paragraphs:
   - **PAS** (Problem → Agitate → Solution) for pages selling against a pain
     point.
   - **AIDA** (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action) for longer landing
     pages with multiple sections.
   - **Feature → Benefit pairs** for product/feature grids: state what it
     does, then what that means for the reader, never just the feature name.
4. **Concrete beats abstract.** Replace vague claims with numbers, names,
   timeframes: "trusted by thousands" → "used by 4,200+ teams"; "fast" →
   "loads in under 1 second"; "we help businesses grow" → name the specific
   outcome. If a real number isn't available, get more specific about the
   mechanism instead of inflating the adjective.
5. **Write scannable, not literary.** Short sentences, short paragraphs (2-3
   lines max on mobile width), bullets for lists of 3+, bold the phrase that
   carries the meaning if a section will be skimmed. Assume F-pattern
   skimming — put the point at the start of the sentence/bullet, not the end.
6. **CTA copy is specific, not generic.** "Get started" is weak; "Start your
   free 14-day trial" or "Get the checklist" tells the reader exactly what
   happens next. One primary CTA per section/screen — don't compete with
   yourself.
7. **Match tone to the site's mood.** Use the same four-mood system as
   `pro-website-builder` (corporate/trustworthy, editorial/elegant, playful/
   creative, technical/minimal) so copy and design read as one voice. A
   playful-mood site with corporate copy (or vice versa) feels off even if
   each is well-written on its own.
8. **Cut on the second pass.** Read it back and remove: filler adverbs
   ("really", "very", "actually"), throat-clearing openers ("In today's
   fast-paced world..."), and any sentence that could apply to a competitor
   unchanged. If it could describe any company in the category, it's not
   doing its job.

## Words and patterns to avoid (unless the brand voice genuinely calls for them)

- Clichés: "game-changer", "unlock your potential", "seamless", "revolutionize",
  "next-level", "cutting-edge", "synergy", "one-stop-shop".
- Hedged claims that say nothing: "one of the best", "a variety of solutions",
  "we care about quality".
- Passive, faceless voice: "issues can be resolved" instead of "we fix it in
  24 hours".
- Stacking three adjectives where one specific noun would do ("innovative,
  powerful, seamless platform" → say what it actually does).

## SEO without stuffing

- Work the primary keyword naturally into the H1 and first paragraph once —
  don't repeat it mechanically through the page.
- Write for the human reading it first; a page that converts but ranks
  slightly lower beats one that ranks but bounces.

## Microcopy

- Form labels: describe what's needed, not just field type ("Work email" not
  "Email").
- Error messages: say what went wrong and what to do, never just "Invalid
  input".
- Empty states / confirmations: reassure and tell the next step ("Check your
  inbox — the guide is on its way" not "Success").
