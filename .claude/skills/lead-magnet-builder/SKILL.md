# Lead magnet builder

Goal: a lead magnet people actually want and actually finish, that leads
naturally into the next step (email list, paid offer, consultation) — not a
generic PDF nobody opens. Pairs with `website-copywriting` (opt-in page copy)
and `pro-website-builder` (opt-in page visual design).

## What makes a lead magnet work

- **One narrow promise, not a broad topic.** "5 email subject lines that get
  40%+ open rates" beats "A Guide to Email Marketing" — narrow scope means it
  can actually be finished and feels immediately useful.
- **Fast to consume.** 5-15 minutes to read/use. A checklist, one-pager,
  template, or short guide (3-8 pages) converts and gets used far more than a
  30-page ebook that sits unread.
- **A quick win, not just theory.** The reader should be able to do or
  produce something by the end, not just "understand" something.
- **A straight line to the next step.** The lead magnet should naturally raise
  the question the paid offer/consultation answers. If someone finishes it
  and there's no obvious next step, the funnel has a gap.
- **Match format to promise:** a process → checklist; a comparison/decision →
  template or scorecard; a concept → short guide; recurring reference →
  cheat sheet/swipe file.

## Process

1. **Confirm the promise before building anything.** Ask: what specific
   result does this guide help someone get, and in how long? If the answer is
   vague ("learn about marketing"), narrow it before writing content.
2. **Structure the guide:**
   - Title = the specific outcome ("The 10-Point Landing Page Audit
     Checklist", not "Landing Page Tips").
   - Short intro: name the problem, promise the outcome, set expectations for
     length/time.
   - Body: the actual steps/checklist/template — the part doing the work.
     Favor bullets, numbered steps, and short blocks over dense paragraphs.
   - Close with a specific next step (not a hard sell): what to do with what
     they just got, and a clear, low-friction link to the next offer.
3. **Build the opt-in page** (use `website-copywriting` for the copy and
   `pro-website-builder` for layout/motion):
   - Headline mirrors the lead magnet's promise exactly — don't make the
     visitor infer what they're getting.
   - 3-5 bullets on what's inside, framed as outcomes not a table of
     contents.
   - A visual of the guide (mockup/cover image) — people trust something they
     can see more than a text description.
   - Minimal form: name + email only unless there's a specific reason to ask
     for more (every extra field drops conversion).
   - One CTA button with copy specific to the deliverable ("Get the
     checklist", not "Submit" or "Download").
   - A one-line privacy/no-spam reassurance near the form if the audience is
     likely privacy-conscious (B2B, health, finance).
4. **Design the delivery moment**, don't leave it as a dead end:
   - Confirmation/thank-you page or message that sets expectation ("Check
     your inbox — it's on its way") and optionally suggests one next action
     (a related page, a follow, a low-friction next offer).
   - If there's a follow-up email sequence, the first email should deliver
     the guide immediately — never make someone wait for the thing they just
     asked for.
5. **Sanity-check before shipping:** would a stranger in the target audience
   actually want this specific thing enough to trade their email for it? If
   the honest answer is "maybe," narrow the promise further rather than
   adding more content.

## Common failure modes to avoid

- Lead magnet topic is broader than what the reader can act on in one
  sitting.
- Opt-in page undersells or oversells relative to what's actually inside
  (mismatch kills trust immediately after signup).
- Asking for phone number/company size/etc. on the opt-in form when email is
  all that's needed to deliver value.
- No clear next step at the end of the guide — the funnel just stops.
